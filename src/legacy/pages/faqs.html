Frequently Asked Questions

Should I just pay my El Paso traffic ticket?

Paying is the quickest option, but it is rarely the best one. In Texas, paying a Class C citation enters a plea and results in a conviction on your driving record. Before you pay, it is worth finding out whether the court will consider a dismissal, deferred disposition or a driving safety course instead. Attorney Robert Navar reviews the citation, your record and the court involved and explains the options, then handles the case for a flat fee. See how to hire us to fight your ticket.

Does paying a ticket count as a conviction?

Yes. Paying the fine on a Texas traffic citation is treated as a plea of guilty or no contest, and the court reports the conviction to the Texas Department of Public Safety. The conviction stays on your driving record, where your insurance carrier may see it. A conviction may affect premiums depending on your history, carrier and other rating factors. Texas no longer uses the former Driver Responsibility Program points system, but repeated moving-violation convictions can still affect your record and may lead to license suspension in some circumstances. What happens if I miss my court date?

If you do not appear or otherwise answer the citation by the appearance date, the court can issue a warrant for your arrest and may file a separate charge for violating your promise to appear. Many El Paso courts also report unresolved cases to the DPS Failure to Appear (OMNI) program, which blocks your license renewal until the court clears the hold. The sooner an attorney contacts the court, the more options remain. See failure to appear warrants and OMNI hold removal.

Can an attorney handle my ticket without me going to court?

In general, yes. For Class C traffic citations an attorney can enter an appearance, file the required paperwork and negotiate with the prosecutor on your behalf. Depending on the court and the outcome, you may not need to attend at all. If a hearing or trial does require your presence, we tell you in advance. Learn more about the traffic citations we handle.

How do I find out which El Paso court has my ticket?

The citation itself names the court. Tickets written by El Paso Police inside the city limits generally go to El Paso Municipal Court. Tickets from DPS troopers and Sheriff's deputies go to the Justice of the Peace precinct for the area of the stop, and tickets from Horizon City, Socorro, San Elizario, Clint or Anthony police go to those city courts. If you lost the ticket, send us your name and date of birth through the ticket and warrant lookup form and we will check the El Paso County courts for you.

Can a traffic warrant be recalled?

Often an attorney can file a motion and post the required bond so the court recalls the warrant and places the case back on the docket, without you being arrested. Warrant recall timing depends on the court's docket; many El Paso courts act within days once an attorney files, but no timeline can be promised. Once the warrant is recalled we work on the underlying ticket itself. Details are on our traffic warrant recall page.

What is an OMNI hold?

OMNI is the DPS Failure to Appear program. When you do not resolve a citation, the court may report you to the program, and DPS will refuse to renew your driver's license until the court clears the hold and the program fee is paid. The hold does not suspend your license, but driving once it expires can lead to a driving while license invalid charge. Resolving the underlying case is what lifts the hold. See OMNI hold removal.

Can a traffic conviction affect my CDL?

Yes, and differently than it affects a regular license. Commercial drivers are generally not eligible for deferred disposition or a driving safety course on a Texas traffic ticket, even when the citation was issued in a personal vehicle, so a conviction is often the only alternative to fighting the case. Certain convictions can lead to CDL disqualification under state and federal rules. If you hold a CDL, do not pay the ticket before speaking with an attorney. See our CDL ticket page.

My license is suspended. Can it be restored?

It depends on why it was suspended. Suspensions tied to unpaid tickets, failure-to-appear holds or an unpaid reinstatement fee can often be cleared by resolving the underlying cases and satisfying DPS requirements. Other suspensions run for a set period. While your license is suspended, do not drive; a new citation for driving while license invalid adds a second case to resolve. What is an occupational driver's license?

An occupational driver's license is a court-ordered restricted license under Texas Transportation Code chapter 521, subchapter L. It lets an eligible driver whose license is suspended drive for work, school and essential household duties, within the limits the judge sets. Obtaining one requires a petition to the proper court, proof of insurance (an SR-22) and a court order that is then filed with DPS. Learn what is involved on our occupational driver's license page.

Can I take defensive driving instead?

Possibly. Deferred disposition and the driving safety course are discretionary options under Texas law with eligibility limits, for example CDL holders, some higher-speed violations and citations without valid insurance are generally excluded, and the request must be made before the appearance date passes. Even when you qualify, it may not be the best route, since it involves court costs, a course and a probation period. We compare it with the other options for your citation.
